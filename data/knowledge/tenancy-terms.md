# Acme Property Management: standard tenancy terms

> **Example terms for a demo.** Acme Property Management is a fictional business, and these terms were written for a
> software demonstration. They are not legal advice and do not summarise any law. A real tenancy is governed by its
> tenancy agreement and the law that applies to it.

These terms sit alongside each tenancy agreement. Where the agreement says something different, the agreement applies.
Replies to tenants refer to a clause like this: (Tenancy terms §7).

## §1 About these terms

"We" means Acme Property Management, which manages the property for its owner. "You" means the tenants named on the
tenancy agreement. These terms explain how we handle the everyday parts of a tenancy: rent, repairs, requests and
moving out. If anything here is unclear, email us and a member of our team will explain it.

## §2 Paying rent

Rent is paid weekly in advance by automatic payment to the account shown on your tenancy agreement. Use the payment
reference on your agreement (your surname and the property code, for example NGATA MTE14) so we can match your payment.
We don't accept cash. If you'd like to pay fortnightly to match your pay cycle, ask us first: a change to how often you
pay needs our written agreement, and fortnightly rent is still paid in advance.

## §3 Late rent and payment arrangements

If you can't pay the full rent on time, tell us before it is due. We will talk through the options with you, which can
include a short payment arrangement. An arrangement only applies once a member of our team confirms it in writing.
Rent that is not paid on the due date is treated as arrears, and we will contact you about it. Rent that stays unpaid
for several weeks can lead to formal steps to end the tenancy, so please talk to us early.

## §4 Receipts, statements and changing how you pay

We send a rent statement on request, covering any period you ask for. If you change banks or the account you pay
from, keep the same payment reference and let us know so we can update our records. If someone else pays the rent for
you, ask them to use your reference. If you pay more than is due, the extra is held as rent in advance unless you ask
for it back; before any refund of an overpayment we check it against the bank statement.

## §5 Rent reviews and changes

The rent only changes by written notice from us. We review the rent no more than once every 12 months and give at
least 60 days' written notice of an increase, stating the new weekly amount and the date it starts. If you want to ask
for a rent reduction, for example because part of the home can't be used, write to us and explain why. The owner
decides, and we confirm the outcome in writing. Nothing changes until that written confirmation.

## §6 Bond

The bond is four weeks' rent. We lodge it with the government bond service and send you the receipt. The bond is held
until the tenancy ends and can't be used to pay rent during the tenancy. How the bond is refunded at the end is set
out in §20.

## §7 Repairs and maintenance

We keep the property in a reasonable state of repair. Report non-urgent repairs by email as soon as you notice them:
say what the problem is, where it is, and attach a photo if you can. We log a maintenance request and arrange a
tradesperson, who contacts you to book a time. Please don't arrange your own tradesperson for non-urgent work unless we
have agreed in writing. You look after the everyday things: keeping the home reasonably clean and tidy, replacing light
bulbs, keeping drains clear and mowing the lawns, unless your agreement says otherwise. Pests that come from the
building, such as rats in the ceiling space, are ours to treat; tell us if they come back after a treatment. Damage
caused by you or your guests, beyond fair wear and tear, may be charged to you.

## §8 Urgent repairs

A repair is urgent when waiting would put people at risk or cause serious damage. Examples: a smell of gas, flooding
or a burst pipe, sparking or burning electrics, water coming through a light fitting, sewage overflowing, no hot
water, a door or window that can't be locked after a break-in, and no heating when a baby, an elderly person or someone
unwell lives in the home. Phone our urgent repairs line (the number is in your tenancy welcome pack) as well as
emailing us. While you wait: if you smell gas, leave the house, don't use switches, and call 111 from outside; for a
burst pipe, turn the water off at the toby; for an electrical fault, switch off at the switchboard if it is safe to do
so; report a break-in to the police. If you can't reach us after reasonable attempts, you may arrange an urgent repair
yourself, and reasonable costs are reimbursed when you send us the invoice.

## §9 Pets

Pets need our written consent before they move in. Tell us the type of animal, its age, and whether it is desexed and
microchipped. The owner decides each request on its merits, and consent can come with conditions, such as professional
carpet cleaning at the end of the tenancy. Fish in a tank don't need consent. Assistance animals don't need consent
either, but please let us know about them.

## §10 Access and inspections

We carry out routine inspections no more than once every four weeks and give at least 48 hours' written notice. For
repairs, we or the tradesperson give at least 24 hours' notice. You don't need to be home: if you are out, we use the
office key and lock up when we leave. We only enter between 8am and 7pm unless you agree to another time. In an
emergency we may enter without notice to protect people or the property.

## §11 Subletting, flatmates and guests

You may not sublet the property or any room, or rent it out through short-term or holiday-rental websites, without our
written consent. If you would like a flatmate to move in, ask us first: they apply like any tenant and, if accepted, are
added to the tenancy agreement or recorded as an occupant. Guests are welcome for short visits. If a guest will stay
longer than two weeks, or the number of people living in the home would go above the maximum on your agreement, ask us
first.

## §12 Ending a periodic tenancy

To end a periodic tenancy, give us at least 21 days' written notice, including the date the tenancy will end. Email is
fine. If you need to leave sooner, ask us: a shorter notice period only applies if we agree to it in writing. Rent is
payable up to and including the last day of the notice period. We contact you to arrange the final inspection and the
return of the keys (see §20).

## §13 Fixed-term tenancies and breaking a lease

A fixed-term tenancy ends on the date in the agreement. Unless either of us gives written notice before that date, it
continues as a periodic tenancy. You can't end a fixed term early on your own, but you can ask us to agree to it, which
is often called breaking the lease. If the owner agrees, you usually keep paying rent until a new tenant moves in and
pay reasonable costs such as advertising. Suggesting a suitable replacement tenant can help. Any agreement to end early
must be in writing.

## §14 Smoke alarms

Every home has long-life photoelectric smoke alarms. Don't remove, cover or disable them, even while cooking. Press the
test button now and then. Tell us straight away if an alarm is missing, damaged or chirping: long-life alarms don't
take new batteries, so a chirp usually means the unit needs replacing, which we arrange.

## §15 Mould, moisture and ventilation

Moisture causes mould, so air the home every day, use the extractor fans when showering and cooking, dry washing
outside or in a vented dryer, and wipe condensation off windows. Clean small spots of mould with a mould cleaner, and
tell us if it comes back or covers a large area so we can look for leaks or ventilation problems. Extractor fans in
bathrooms and kitchens are ours to repair; report a faulty fan like any other repair.

## §16 Alterations, painting and fixtures

You can use removable adhesive hooks without asking. Anything that makes holes or changes the property, such as nails,
screws, shelves, painting, wallpaper, aerials or changes to the garden, needs our written consent first. The owner
won't unreasonably refuse a minor change, but may ask you to use a professional and to put things back at the end of
the tenancy. Changes made without consent may have to be reversed at your cost.

## §17 Noise and neighbours

Keep noise at a level that doesn't disturb the neighbours, especially between 10pm and 7am. You are responsible for
the behaviour of your guests. If a neighbour is disturbing you, a polite conversation is often the quickest fix. If
that doesn't work, or you feel unsafe, send us the dates, the times and what happened, and we will follow it up. For
threats or violence, call the police first. We can't share details about other tenants, but we do act on complaints
about our own tenants.

## §18 Keys, locks and security

We give you keys for every external door and every window lock. Don't change or add locks without our consent; if we
agree, give us a copy of the new key. If you lose a key or lock yourself out, contact us during office hours to borrow
the office set, or call a locksmith at your own cost after hours. A lock broken in a break-in is an urgent repair
(see §8).

## §19 Contents insurance and damage

The owner insures the building, but that insurance doesn't cover your belongings. We strongly recommend contents
insurance that includes liability cover for accidental damage to the property. If something is damaged, tell us
straight away, whoever caused it. Careless damage may be charged to you within the limits set by law, and deliberate
damage is charged in full.

## §20 Moving out, final inspection and bond refund

Before you leave, clean the home to the standard it was in when you moved in, allowing for fair wear and tear, remove
your belongings and rubbish, and return every key. Professional carpet cleaning is only needed if your agreement or
pet consent requires it, or if the carpets are dirtier than at the start. We do the final inspection with you on or
after your last day. Once the inspection is done and all rent is paid, we sign the bond refund form with you and the
bond service pays the refund directly to you. If we claim any of the bond, we explain why in writing.

## §21 How we communicate

Email is the best way to reach us about anything that isn't urgent; for urgent repairs, phone us as well (see §8).
Please tell us when your phone number or email address changes. Only a written confirmation from a member of our team
agrees to anything, such as consent, a payment arrangement or a change to your tenancy. We keep your information
private and share it only with tradespeople and others who need it to manage your tenancy.
